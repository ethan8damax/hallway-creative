# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Prospective clients — engaged couples, sports teams/athletes, event organizers — researching photographers/videographers to hire. They arrive from a referral, social, or search, and need to believe Andrew's work is high-quality and diverse before they'll reach out. Secondary user: Andrew himself, who self-serves content updates through the `/admin` dashboard without needing a code change.

## Product Purpose

Convince a visitor that Andrew's photo/video work is high quality and diverse enough that they want access to it, and make it effortless for them to then reach out and hire him. Success is a submitted contact form from a visitor who's already sold by the portfolio.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, confident, understated. Simple, professional, yet immersive. The photography is the star — the interface recedes and never competes with the work for attention.

## Anti-references

- Generic photographer-template clichés: stock-photo hero carousels, cursor-follow gimmicks, testimonial-carousel walls.
- SaaS-marketing-site grammar transplanted onto a portfolio: hero-metric stat blocks, "trusted by" logo rows, feature-grid cards.
- AI-slop scaffolding: tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, gradient text, side-stripe accent borders, numbered 01/02/03 section markers used as decoration rather than a real sequence, identical icon+heading+text card grids.
- Anything that reads as "confident" through loudness (oversized type, saturated color covering most of the surface) rather than through restraint.

## Design Principles

- Photography carries the page. UI is quiet by default; it never competes with the work.
- One confident accent color, used rarely and deliberately (the "tally light" — the red recording indicator on a video camera). Its scarcity is what makes it read as confident, not decorative.
- Dark is the resting state (the screening room). Light mode exists as a fully realized second surface for daytime/print-adjacent contexts, carrying the identical identity — not a stripped-down fallback.
- Simple before clever. Every section earns its place; nothing gets added because "portfolio sites do this."

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large text ≥3:1, full keyboard navigation, and `prefers-reduced-motion` support (crossfade/instant fallback) on every choreographed reveal. No additional known requirements.
